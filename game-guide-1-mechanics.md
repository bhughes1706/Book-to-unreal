# Buzzing Electric — Game Guide, Part 1: Mechanics & Integration

Governing principle for every system in this document: **nothing is "a game mechanic." Everything is a thing that exists in Utopia**, exactly as it exists in the book, and the player interacts with it the way Grayson does. There is no non-diegetic UI, no abstract meter, no system without a fictional body. Every mechanic below lists its source in the book, how it plays, and what it feeds. The last section is the integration map — the mechanics are designed as one organism, and any change to one should be checked against that map.

---

## 1. The Ledger (credits)

**In the fiction:** crypto-credits are Utopia's only currency, moved by Bracelet wave. Income arrives from the state (basic income, gated by compliance), from Grayson's royalties (the book, then Daniil's 5% merchandise deal), and eventually from the state's Directive Bonus economy paying parents for compliance with the book. The state's fines, payments, and payouts all move through the same rail. (Sources: Bracelet income posters Ch1; the 5,000-credit trespass fine Ch4; 2,000 to buy back the book and 3,000 fine for burning it, Ch6; 5,000 given to Trofim Ch8; suicide-credit payouts throughout.)

**How it plays:** credits are the game's resolution system — the replacement for combat. Every dramatic beat resolves through a transaction: paying, refusing to pay, giving, being fined, being paid *at* (royalties). Transactions are deliberate inputs with weight — the Bracelet-wave gesture is the game's "attack animation," with a beat of confirmation on anything meaningful. Spending is always characterization: the wallet is the moral ledger, and the game deliberately rhymes its numbers (the amount the state fines you is the amount you can give away) so that every figure the player sees twice means something.

**Key sub-systems:**
- **Basic income** — deposited on schedule *only* while compliance holds. The first thing the decay spiral cuts.
- **The royalty ticker** — passive deposits that arrive unprompted, forever, as strangers buy the book and its merchandise. After the mid-game Directive Bonus escalation, each tick is a fraction of a verified compliance event somewhere on Earth. The player cannot turn it off. One optional late-game screen — the itemized royalty statement — shows what the ticks are (categories, regions, counts; textual/numeric only, per the redlines).
- **The transaction log** — player-readable, and identical to what the state reads. The log is simultaneously the player's history and the surveillance record: everything bought is remembered, which makes purchasing itself a stealth consideration (see Suspicion).
- **Futile gestures** — a protected verb class: acts of resistance that cost credits, raise suspicion, and change nothing systemic (burning the book, overpaying a stranger, asking for another fine). The game permits them everywhere they'd make sense, precisely because they're the only free choices Utopia sells.

**Feeds:** Compliance (income gating), Suspicion (log analysis), the Bracelet (transaction instrument), Rae (Act 3 — feeding an unregistered person costs double and shows in the log).

## 2. The Life Bracelet

**In the fiction:** the state's wearable — identity, wallet, vitals monitor, exercise tracker, and legal existence in one band. No Bracelet, no personhood: no purchases, no Rails, no doors, no record. Children wear them like everyone else, which is how the state's telemetry reaches into every home. (Sources: income/exercise posters Ch1; Bracelet ads Ch2; the quota ping during the skyscraper breakdown Ch3; the fine delivered by scan Ch4; Ella's bare wrist Ch4; the spare Bracelet gifted Ch3 and feared Ch4.)

**How it plays:** the Bracelet is the body's interface with every other system —
- **Quota:** a rolling movement requirement (distance/activity per day). Met: basic income flows. Missed: income throttles. The quota is satisfied by *actually moving through the world*, which welds the economy to traversal — walking to a witness event pays rent. The game's darkest tutorial beat is already in the book: the quota congratulating Grayson mid-breakdown as he climbs a skyscraper drunk.
- **Vitals/health monitoring:** heart rate, sleep, intoxication — logged, not gamified. There is no health bar; there is a *state that watches your health*. Anomalous vitals (panic at a witness event, days of drinking, sleepless nights) don't damage the player — they generate *data*, and data generates attention: a wellness-check appointment, a kiosk recommendation pushed to the Lens, a solicitous message. The player's body is an informant they live inside.
- **Transactions:** every payment, fine, and transfer is a wrist gesture. Physical, visible, public.
- **The spare Bracelet:** the dead band gifted in Ch3 sits in the inventory all game — unexplained, slightly illegal, feared by Ella — until Act 3, when an unregistered child needs an identity. Longest-planted payoff in the game.

**Feeds:** Ledger (instrument), Compliance (quota), Suspicion (vitals anomalies), Health systems (monitoring), Rae (the spare).

## 3. The Lens

**In the fiction:** the AR layer on everyone's eyes — translation, search, labels, feeds, state messaging. Active even when eyes are closed. Disconnects in dead zones and can be cut by the state (the white room). (Sources: wake-up projection Ch2; subtitles and labels throughout; the Deciphering Machines app purchase Ch3; the bibliography paywall/error Ch4; the interrogation disconnect Ch5.)

**How it plays:** **the Lens is the entire UI.** Every element the player sees — meters, labels, waypoints, subtitles, notifications — is diegetically the Lens, which gives the interface three properties no conventional HUD has:

- **It can be withdrawn.** Dead zones and state rooms drop the player to raw, unlabeled world. The game's meaning-arc rides on this: HUD-absent-outside (the beach: peace) vs. HUD-absent-inside (the white room: captivity) vs. the endgame (the Lens dying by degrees on the road out — liberation). Three valences of the same silence.
- **It can lie.** Labels serve the state: a person the player needs reads "VAGRANT — DO NOT ENGAGE"; a truth the player seeks returns paywalls, wrong languages, and errors on files it just sold them (the Ch4 bibliography scene is the tutorial for distrusting your own interface).
- **It can be extended.** The **app store**: purchasable Lens programs (deciphering, scanning, profile tools) bought with credits — every capability upgrade is also a logged purchase, so the player's toolkit and their surveillance exposure grow in the same transaction.

**The notification stack** is the Lens's dramatic instrument and the game's heartbeat. Standing notifications, all ignorable, all remembered:
- **The Selection icon** — blinks monthly with the state's canonized book. Ignoring it is Grayson's "rebellion-lite," performable by the player without being told it's characterization; the system logs non-engagement.
- **The royalty ticker** — see Ledger.
- **Adelaide's calls** — routed through Noah, then direct: the un-declined decline. The dark twin of the Selection icon: two blinking notifications, one from the state he should refuse, one from her that he shouldn't. Answering is one of the longest-deferred choices in the game.
- **State solicitations** — wellness-check summons, kiosk pushes, survey requests. The polite face of Suspicion.

**Search & query laundering:** the Lens search is real and logged. Suspicious queries ("remove Life Bracelet") raise the profile; the countermeasure — from the book — is burying signal in noise: mundane, embarrassing, high-volume queries and purchases that statistically launder the log. This is the game's stealth system. It contains no crouching.

**Feeds:** every system — the Lens is the membrane through which all of them touch the player.

## 4. Compliance

**In the fiction:** the state's continuous assessment of whether a citizen is functioning as designed — exercised, solvent, regular, engaged.

**How it plays:** a rolling 7-day score fed by quota performance, purchase regularity, sleep patterns, and feed engagement. It is a *credit rating, not a wanted level*: high compliance means income flows, Rails open, dispensers vend, and the player is boring. Low compliance triggers no pursuit — it triggers **decay**: income throttles, then stops; services decline politely; the fine-cascade compounds. There are no fail states, only spirals — bottoming out doesn't end the game, it makes the city stop serving the player until digging out consumes days. The punishment is the life.

**Feeds:** Ledger (income), Rails (access), vendor interactions, and — inverted — Suspicion (a *too*-perfect profile after a suspicious event is itself a flag; the systems disagree with each other, which is the design's engine).

## 5. Suspicion (the Profile)

**In the fiction:** the state's *interest* in a citizen, as distinct from their compliance. Compliance asks "are you a good citizen"; Suspicion asks "are you an interesting one."

**How it plays:** raised by anomalies — restricted queries, double food purchases, unmetered movement, proximity to Eater zones or unregistered persons, vitals spikes at the wrong moments, and *fame incidents* (see Recognition). Lowered by laundering (see Lens) and by time spent boring. High suspicion summons nothing with a weapon; it summons **attention**: a mandatory wellness interview, a Strong Inc. representative who'd love a moment, a survey that isn't optional. These interviews are the game's boss encounters — Papers-Please-style performances of normalcy conducted through the same Q&A interface the prologue taught as comedy, now inverted. Answers move meters and characterize Grayson simultaneously; the state asks the expository questions, so the interviews are also the game's only cutscene-equivalent.

**Feeds:** the interview set-pieces, the Ledger (laundering costs money), Act 3 (smuggling lives or dies on the profile).

## 6. Health, Medication & the Wellness Apparatus

**In the fiction:** the state medicalizes what it manufactures — kiosks on every corner dispensing mood by category ("Feelings of Sadness or Depression"), suicide-credit registration marketed beside them, wellness as the state's love language. (Sources: kiosks and the crushed-man gate Ch4; Combinations referenced Ch4/Ch8 — cut from the game per earlier ruling; suicide-credit registration desks and payouts throughout.)

**How it plays:**
- **Vitals are watched, not managed** — see Bracelet. The player never fills a health bar; they generate a medical record.
- **Kiosk modifiers:** purchasable mood compounds with one honest, diegetic effect — the *world's color grade shifts* (the Ch4 warm-wash rendered literally). No stat buffs; the drug changes what the city looks like, which is what it did in the book. Purchases logged; the state notices patterns and responds with recommendations, not judgment — which is worse.
- **The suicide-credit economy is ambient and ever-present, never a player option.** Registration desks exist in the world; payouts run on tickers; other citizens' Bracelets flatline nearby and the city does not pause (the Ch1 diver is the pattern: a witness event the player can miss, acknowledged only by a credit-release notice). Its function is atmospheric and moral — the state's floor price for a life, always posted — and it stays firmly in the institutional layer per the redlines.

**Feeds:** Suspicion (vitals anomalies, purchase patterns), Witnessing (flatline events), tone.

## 7. The Manuscript (writing as quest structure)

**In the fiction:** Noah's park-bench directive — *write about her* — becomes the second book, written chapter by chapter through the story's middle. The rewrite's own structure (present-tense frame, past-tense memory chapters) is the system's blueprint. (Sources: Ch6 ending; Ch7 as the first memory chapter.)

**How it plays:** the game's spine-quest and flashback engine in one. Grayson can sit and write at designated stations (hotel desk, park bench); each writing session opens a **playable memory** — the Ohio house, daylight, no neon, no HUD (the Lens has nothing to label there), small domestic interactions in a palette the rest of the game never uses. But each memory must be *unlocked*: Grayson can't write a chapter until something in the present rhymes with it — a line of Trofim's, an object found, a witness event that lands near the wound. This is **resonance foraging**, and it's what aims the open acts: the player moves through the world partly hunting the courage to remember. The memories are player-authored in fiction — what the player plays is what Grayson writes down — which sets up the arc's gravest beat: when the apparatus eventually takes this manuscript too, it confiscates hours the player personally lived in that house.

**Feeds:** Act structure (quest spine), the Adelaide reframe (the memories are where her truth lives), the endgame.

## 8. Witnessing (attention as a verb)

**In the fiction:** the book's signature scenes are watched, not fought — the diver, Dale, the stoning, the old man with the printed book. The city's crowds move past all of it. (Sources: Ch1, Ch5, Ch8, Ch3.)

**How it plays:** witness events fire in the world *whether or not the player is looking*. Noticing is the mechanic: approach, watch, look away, act within the scene's small verb set (bandage, buy, give, run). Never marked as content — no objective pop, no journal, no completion tick. The design rations agency per scene to what the scene's meaning can survive: some are scripted failure (Dale — you cannot save him), some are controllable failure (the stoning — run, genuinely, and arrive late), and the escalation across the game is itself the arc: somewhere ahead is the scene where intervention finally works, and it's Rae. What the player saw and didn't see becomes state the world quietly remembers.

**Feeds:** the Manuscript (witness events are resonance sources), Suspicion (lingering at the wrong scenes), the children thread, Act 3's emotional load.

## 9. Recognition (fame as exposure)

**In the fiction:** Grayson is the most famous author alive, and being seen is never neutral — fans, accusers, a man who thanks him for the courage to abandon a child, an old man who never read it. (Sources: Ch3 "Leading Critic" rebranding; Ch5 airport; Ch8 Trofim.)

**How it plays:** NPCs recognize Grayson stochastically — each encounter a ten-second characterization delivery *and* a systems event (crowds gather, feeds mention location, Suspicion ticks: fame makes surveillance worse). The player learns to manage visibility the way other games manage noise. Rare inverse encounters — the ones who don't know, or don't care — read as sanctuary, and the game spends them deliberately (Trofim). Defiance in public doesn't reduce fame; the state *absorbs* it (the Ch3 lesson: his breakdown becomes "Leading Critic" content by nightfall).

**Feeds:** Suspicion, Witnessing (recognized-Grayson changes some scenes), tone.

## 10. The Eaters (the clock)

**In the fiction:** autonomous reclamation machines converting the human world back to wilderness, expanding at a visible, scheduled pace, morally neutral and unstoppable. (Sources: manuscript; tickers and anniversary content in the rewrite.)

**How it plays:** the campaign clock and the map's editor. Districts on the city's edge are consumed on a schedule announced on every ticker — consumed districts are *gone*, including their vendors, witness events, and unvisited content. The player will lose things and should feel it. The advancing edge is also the game's standing horizon image (wilderness eating neon) and, in the endgame, the direction of exit: the dead place lies beyond where the Eaters have already finished.

**Feeds:** act pacing, triage pressure on the open acts, the ending's geography, theme (Adelaide's "I want to shake with nature" — the Eaters are building what she was starving for).

## 11. Rails & Mobility

**In the fiction:** high-speed transit, Bracelet-gated; entire places exist that no Rail reaches — and reaching them is a class privilege or a pilgrimage on foot (Trofim's wife's grave, thirty miles out). (Sources: Ch2, Ch6, Ch8.)

**How it plays:** Rails are fast travel with conditions — fare (Ledger), standing (Compliance), and logging (every ride recorded). The map's deeper structure is what Rails *don't* serve: off-Rail zones cost time and shoe leather, generate no telemetry (the only unlogged movement in the game), and hold the content the state has priced out of reach. The Trofim graveyard walk is the tutorial: the first time the player travels somewhere the system isn't watching, and the first rehearsal for the endgame exodus — which is one long off-Rail journey out of the world.

**Feeds:** Ledger, Compliance, Suspicion (unlogged movement is invisible *and* its absence is conspicuous), endgame.

## 12. Rae & the Smuggling Inversion (Act 3)

**In the fiction:** the unregistered child — no Bracelet, no record, no legal existence; the one person the system cannot see, feed, transport, or monetize; collected at six o'clock if found. (Sources: manuscript; Dale and the collection schedule Ch5.)

**How it plays:** Rae's arrival transforms every mastered system without adding a new interface —
- **Ledger:** two mouths, one registered. Double food purchases distort the log.
- **Doors/Rails/dispensers:** she can't use any of them; every gated threshold becomes a smuggling problem solved with distraction, spoofing, or the spare Bracelet's long-planted payoff.
- **Suspicion:** the profile now protects two people; laundering becomes life-support.
- **Witnessing inverted:** after a game of watching children the player couldn't save, one is *theirs* — the intervention that finally works, made of every mechanic the game taught.
- **Her own meter — trust** — moves on choices (lying to her, hiding her too long, what she's allowed to see), not proximity; it gates the ending, never moment-to-moment babysitting. She is also capability: unmonitored, she goes where Grayson's profile can't.

**Feeds:** everything — Act 3 is the systems inheriting the story.

## 13. The Integration Map

The organism, traced: **movement** earns **quota** (Bracelet) which sustains **Compliance** which releases **income** (Ledger) which buys **food, fares, apps, and laundering** — but purchases feed the **log**, the log feeds **Suspicion**, Suspicion summons **interviews**, and surviving interviews costs **Compliance-perfect performance**. Meanwhile the **Lens** delivers all of it and lies about some of it; **fame** multiplies exposure; **vitals** inform on the body; the **royalty ticker** pays the player with the catastrophe's growth; **witness events** wound the player into **writing**, writing opens the **memories**, and the memories assemble the truth about Adelaide. The **Eaters** delete map on a schedule; the **Rails** log every shortcut and refuse the places that matter. Then **Rae** arrives and every loop above acquires a second heartbeat it must hide.

Design law derived from the map: **no system may be tuned in isolation.** Every meter touches at least three others by design; that interlock — pressure from connection, not from difficulty — is the game. Any proposed mechanic that doesn't connect to at least two existing systems in this map should justify its existence or be cut.
