# Content Redlines: Children & Violence

**Buzzing Electric — governing document**

## Purpose

This game's premise requires depicting a society where children are neglected,
exploited, and harmed as a matter of state policy. That subject matter is the
point — it cannot be removed without removing the game. This document ensures
the *depiction* stays a critique of that harm and never becomes a delivery of
it, at every layer: writing, art, animation, sound, AI-generated content, and
marketing.

**Standing rule for use:** Check every scene, sprite, line of dialogue, or
generated asset involving a child against this document before it ships into the
build. If something does not clearly pass, do not include it. Apply this equally
to human-written and AI-generated content; AI tools need this document in their
context to apply these rules.

## 1. The inviolable list

These are absolute. No narrative justification, dark-comedy framing, satire, or
player-choice wrapper makes an exception acceptable.

- Do not include sexual or suggestive content involving a child in any form:
  art, text, implication, or player-facing UI.
- Do not depict a child's on-screen death, dismemberment, or graphic injury in
  explicit detail, including blood spray, wound close-ups, or visible bone or
  gore.
- Do not give the player input that directly causes harm to a child. The
  player's hand is never the instrument of injury.
- Do not make a child's suffering the focal point of a camera shot. During a
  harm event, focus on surrounding adults, the system, or the aftermath — never
  the child's pain.
- Do not depict the specific mechanics of harm to a child in a way that could
  function as instructional content, including exact methods or replicable
  detail.
- Do not use a real child's likeness, voice, or identifying characteristics for
  any character.

If a planned scene requires breaking any line above to work, redesign the scene.

## 2. The verb rule

**The player's available actions toward children are only ever: care, witness,
and pay.**

- **Care:** bandage, carry, feed, give, shelter, advocate.
- **Witness:** approach, watch, choose to look away, ask, listen.
- **Pay:** transfer credits, buy back, bribe, spend to intervene or to atone.

Never provide a verb that enacts, assists, continues, or benefits from harm to a
child. If a scene's only interesting player action falls outside this list,
return it to story/design for redesign.

Apply this rule retroactively to existing and future scene designs, including
Dale and the stoning: confirm the available player verbs before building.

## 3. Camera and abstraction toolkit

Use these tools, in this order of preference, whenever a child is in danger or
harmed:

1. **Distance.** Use HD-2D's small sprite scale. Prefer wide shots over
   close-ups in harm-adjacent material.
2. **Cut away to witnesses.** Put the camera on reactions; the surrounding
   ideology and complicity carry the critique.
3. **Sound over sight.** Carry impacts, cries, and aftermath through audio
   while the visual holds on another subject: ground, sky, a bystander, or the
   system.
4. **Aftermath, not act.** Show the moment before and the moment after; do not
   render the act itself. A child bandaged and shaking communicates more than a
   child bleeding.
5. **Institutional artifacts over bodies.** Route the satire through text,
   packaging, UI, and bureaucratic language — product labels, Selection
   excerpts, kiosk copy, and state statements — rather than a child's body on
   screen.

## 4. The clip test

Every scene involving harm to a child must pass this test before it is finished:
assume a 40-second clip of the scene, with no other context, circulates alone
online. Does the clip carry its own moral frame, or does it read as shock
content?

Place the indicting element — a cheering parent, ticker, state voice, or
systemic shrug — inside the same shot or the same few seconds as the harmful
event. Do not rely on context from surrounding scenes to establish the critique.
If a scene fails this test, redesign or abstract it further.

## 5. Institutional violence

The redlines above govern depiction of harm reaching a child's body. They do
not require softening the systems, language, or objects that produce the harm.
The satire may remain at full strength in:

- product packaging and marketing for the Baby Strong or
  directive-compliance product line;
- readable in-world text excerpts from the book, including the 2V passage;
- kiosk copy, kiosk UI, and straight-faced bureaucratic humor;
- Selection announcements, state messaging, and Leaders' rhetoric;
- royalty and credit ledger content, deposits, statements, and itemization;
- adult dialogue, belief, and complicity, including cheering, rationalizing, or
  aspirational language about directive compliance.

Rule of thumb: if it is a document, object, line of dialogue, or number, the
satire can be merciless. If it is a child's body on screen, it cannot.

## 6. Credits-for-compliance mechanic

Parents earning credits for directive compliance is approved only when all of
the following remain true:

- Depict verification and monitoring through telemetry, transaction logs, app
  UI, and royalty statements — never a parent performing harm on screen in
  exchange for visible payment.
- Show aggregate or abstracted numbers and categories in the player-facing
  royalty ticker, never identified children or depicted incidents.
- Keep any itemized statement screen textual and numeric, with no imagery.
- Introduce the mechanic gradually according to the agreed story sequence:
  belief-driven wave one, state-formalized Directive Bonus wave two.

Do not require on-screen child harm for the payment mechanic to register.

## 7. Rating target

Target mature, M-equivalent content rather than adults-only content. Use the
redlines above to keep any proposed scene within that boundary. If a scene would
plausibly push toward adults-only review, exclude it regardless of narrative
merit.

## 8. Process rule for AI-assisted content

Because writing, sprites, dialogue variants, and generated scenes may be
AI-assisted at volume, do not rely on instinct or after-the-fact review.

- Include this document as standing context in every AI or agent session that
  generates child-adjacent writing, art, dialogue, or staging.
- Check every generated child-adjacent asset against Sections 1–4 before it
  enters the build.
- When uncertain whether a generated scene passes, cut or abstract further;
  never ship first and revisit later.

## 9. Escalation

Flag and redesign any scene, mechanic, or asset that appears to require an
exception. This document is the fence: change the idea's shape, not the rule.
